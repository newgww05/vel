Chapter 01 · The Product

A Clear Window With
Protection Underneath

Velcura's PICC Securement Dressing is a transparent polyurethane film dressing with an integrated chlorhexidine gluconate (CHG) gel pad that sits directly over the catheter insertion site. It is designed for PICC, CVC and other central lines where reducing catheter-related bloodstream infection is part of the care bundle.

It closes a long-standing trade-off in vascular access. A plain transparent film lets nurses see the insertion site but offers no antimicrobial activity underneath. A gauze dressing or a CHG foam disc adds antimicrobial protection but hides the site, so checking it means removing the dressing. Here, both the film and the gel are transparent: the site, hub and proximal catheter stay visible for the whole wear time.

Chapter 03 · Evidence

Why CHG at the
Insertion Site

Most catheter-related bloodstream infections in short-term central lines start with skin organisms migrating along the outside of the catheter from the insertion site. Putting a sustained antiseptic exactly at that point is the logic behind CHG-impregnated dressings.

Randomized trial

Timsit et al., 2012

In a multicenter randomized trial in critically ill adults, CHG-gel transparent dressings reduced catheter-related infections compared with standard dressings.

Am J Respir Crit Care Med
Guideline

CDC recommendation

CDC recommends CHG-impregnated dressings for short-term, non-tunneled central venous catheters in adult patients at risk, as part of the central-line bundle.

Category IA (2017 update)
Practice standards

SHEA & INS

SHEA strategies and the Infusion Therapy Standards of Practice include CHG dressings among measures to prevent CLABSI.

Bundle component
Bundle reminder: A dressing is one element of a central-line bundle. Hand hygiene, maximal barrier precautions at insertion, CHG-alcohol skin antisepsis, daily review of line necessity and prompt removal remain the foundation.
Chapter 04 · Use

Application, Wear Time
and Cautions

  1. Prepare the skin with an alcohol-based antiseptic per protocol and let it dry completely.
  2. Center the CHG gel pad over the insertion site, seat the catheter in the keyhole notch, and apply the film without stretching.
  3. Seal the reinforced border and remove the application frame; label with the date.
  4. Inspect through the dressing at every assessment for redness, exudate, displacement or edge-lift.
  5. Change per institutional policy — typically every 7 days when intact — or sooner if the dressing is loose, soiled, damp or the site needs direct assessment.
Caution 01

CHG sensitivity

Do not use in patients with known chlorhexidine hypersensitivity. Remove and assess if irritation or allergic reaction appears.

Caution 02

Neonates and preterm infants

Fragile, immature skin can react to CHG. Follow neonatal protocols and the institutional CHG policy.

Caution 03

Not a treatment

The dressing is designed to reduce infection risk at the insertion site; it does not treat an established catheter infection.

Caution 04

Plain film still has a role

For routine peripheral lines, a plain transparent film is usually sufficient and more economical.

For non-antimicrobial securement, see the transparent IV securement dressing and the frame-style film with notch.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a CHG gel pad dressing?
A transparent film dressing with a chlorhexidine gluconate gel pad that sits over the catheter insertion site, providing continuous antimicrobial activity while keeping the site visible.
How long can the PICC Securement Dressing stay on?
It provides CHG antimicrobial activity for up to 7 days. Change it per institutional policy — typically every 7 days when intact — or sooner if loose, soiled, damp or if the site needs direct assessment.
Which catheters does it fit?
PICC, CVC (subclavian, jugular, femoral), midline, arterial and short peripheral IV catheters up to 12 French, secured through a keyhole notch without sutures.
Is there evidence that CHG dressings reduce bloodstream infections?
Yes. A 2012 multicenter randomized trial in critically ill adults found CHG-gel dressings reduced catheter-related infections, and CDC recommends CHG-impregnated dressings for short-term non-tunneled central venous catheters in adults.
Can it be supplied under a private label?
Yes. It is individually sterile-packed with an application frame, and OEM packaging is available. Contact Velcura for MOQ, documents and registration status in your market.
