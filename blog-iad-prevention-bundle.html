The Five-Step
IAD Prevention Bundle

Incontinence-associated dermatitis is driven by moisture, irritants and friction at the same time — so no single product prevents it. Here is a five-step bundle that addresses all three, the product that fits each step, how to match it to GLOBIAD severity, and a checklist that works on any shift.

Key Takeaways

Five Things to Know in Two Minutes

  1. IAD = moisture + irritants + friction. A bundle addresses all three together.
  2. Order matters: cleanse first, then moisturize and protect — never protect over residue.
  3. One breathable absorbent layer, changed promptly; never stack plastic-backed pads.
  4. Use GLOBIAD (Category 1 or 2, A or B) to document and escalate consistently.
  5. Standardize products on the ward and treat the cause of incontinence, not only the skin.
Chapter 01 · Why a Bundle

Five Steps,
Done Every Time

Incontinence-associated dermatitis (IAD) is driven by three forces acting together: moisture that over-hydrates the skin, chemical irritants in urine and stool, and friction on that weakened skin. Any single measure addresses one force. A bundle — a small set of steps performed together, every time — addresses all three.

The Global IAD Expert Panel built its prevention advice around a structured skin-care regimen: cleanse, protect, and (where needed) restore. The five-step bundle below operationalizes that regimen for a working shift, and adds the two steps that make it last: moisture management and repositioning. For the diagnosis itself, see IAD vs pressure injury.

Chapter 03 · Severity

Match the Bundle to
the Category

GLOBIAD 1

Persistent redness

Skin is red but intact. Full bundle, with focus on prompt cleansing and a protectant at every change. Find and treat the cause of incontinence.

GLOBIAD 2

Skin loss

Erosion, denudation or blisters. Gentle cleansing, a protectant suited to broken skin (for example a barrier film), and clinical review.

Treat and protect
Any category · B

Signs of infection

Suspected fungal (often Candida) or bacterial infection needs assessment and specific treatment alongside the bundle.

Escalate

The Ghent Global IAD Categorisation Tool (GLOBIAD) classifies IAD as Category 1 (persistent redness) or Category 2 (skin loss), each with "A" (no clinical signs of infection) or "B" (signs of infection). Using the same tool on every shift makes documentation consistent — and shows whether the bundle is working.

Chapter 04 · Operationalize

A Shift-Ready
Checklist

  1. Check the skin at every continence care episode and at least once per shift — perineum, buttocks, groin, inner thighs, sacrum.
  2. Cleanse soon after every episode — stool on skin is the most damaging exposure; minutes matter.
  3. Protect after every cleanse, using the protectant chosen for this patient's skin condition.
  4. One breathable absorbent layer only; change when wet, and check the size fits.
  5. Reposition on schedule and use the turn to look at the skin.
  6. Document the GLOBIAD category and any change, and escalate Category 2 or any sign of infection.
Barrier 01

"We don't have time."

No-rinse cleansing and leave-on barrier films typically save time compared with basin washes and cream removal. The bundle is designed to be faster.

Barrier 02

Different products on every shift

Standardize one cleanser and one or two protectants on the ward. Consistency is what makes a bundle a bundle.

Barrier 03

No documentation

Without a category recorded each day, nobody can tell whether skin is improving. Use GLOBIAD on the chart.

Barrier 04

Treating the skin, not the cause

Assess and manage the incontinence itself — infection, medication, mobility, toileting — or the bundle runs forever.

Small Steps, Every Single Time

IAD prevention is not complicated; it is repetitive. Five steps, done in order at every episode, protect skin that would otherwise break down within days.

The right products make the bundle faster: no-rinse cleansers, leave-on protectants and breathable, washable bedding that keeps the skin drier between changes.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an IAD prevention bundle?
A small set of skin-care steps performed together at every continence care episode: cleanse, moisturize, protect, manage moisture and reposition. Together they address moisture, chemical irritants and friction.
What is the best skin protectant for IAD?
It depends on skin condition. Acrylate barrier films suit intact or damaged skin and need no removal; dimethicone and zinc-oxide products are widely used on intact or reddened skin. Follow local formularies and product instructions.
What is GLOBIAD?
The Ghent Global IAD Categorisation Tool. It classifies IAD as Category 1 (persistent redness) or Category 2 (skin loss), each subdivided into A (no clinical signs of infection) or B (signs of infection).
How often should skin be cleansed in incontinence care?
Soon after every episode of incontinence, especially stool, using a pH-balanced no-rinse cleanser, followed by a skin protectant.
Should multiple underpads be used for heavy incontinence?
No. Stacking several plastic-backed pads traps heat and moisture and worsens the skin microclimate. Use one breathable absorbent product of the right capacity and change it promptly.
