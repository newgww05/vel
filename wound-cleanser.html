The Step Before Every Dressing

No dressing performs to its full potential on top of yesterday's debris. Before a hydrogel can rehydrate, before an alginate can gel, before a silver dressing can control bioburden — the wound bed has to be clean. It's the least glamorous step in wound care, and the one most often rushed.

Velcura Wound Cleanser is a pH-balanced, sterile irrigation solution built specifically for this moment. Its surfactant formula lifts debris, slough and loosely adherent bacteria away from the wound bed — gently enough for daily use, without the cytotoxicity concerns that come with routine antiseptic use.

pH-Balanced Formula

Formulated close to the wound's natural physiological pH, minimising irritation and supporting rather than disrupting the healing environment.

Removes Debris Without Trauma

Surfactant action lifts debris, slough and biofilm from the wound bed without the mechanical scrubbing that can damage fragile new tissue.

Suitable Across Wound Types

One formula for chronic ulcers, acute traumatic wounds, burns and routine post-operative wound care — one product to standardise on.

Controlled-Flow Application

A squeeze bottle with a controlled-flow nozzle delivers a precise, hygienic stream — no separate applicator, no guesswork on volume.

Cleanser, Saline or Antiseptic?

All three touch the wound bed at some point in care, but they are not interchangeable. Confusing them is one of the most common — and most avoidable — missteps in routine wound management.

Frequently Asked Questions

Common Questions on
Wound Cleanser

What is the difference between a wound cleanser and saline?
Saline is safe and physiological, but it has no surfactants, so it relies on mechanical flushing alone and struggles to lift adherent debris, slough or biofilm. A surfactant-based wound cleanser loosens and lifts this material without added mechanical force, and typically includes preservatives that give it a longer shelf life once opened — saline is generally recommended for use within 24 hours of opening.
Is wound cleanser the same as an antiseptic?
No. Antiseptics such as povidone-iodine, chlorhexidine or hydrogen peroxide are antimicrobial but can be cytotoxic to healthy cells with repeated use, and some clinical guidance discourages their routine use on clean granulating wounds because they may slow healing. A pH-balanced wound cleanser is designed for routine, repeated use at every dressing change without that trade-off.
When should a wound be cleansed before dressing?
At every dressing change. Cleansing removes residual dressing material, exudate, debris and loose slough so the clinician can accurately assess the wound bed and so the new dressing makes full contact with viable tissue rather than sitting on top of debris.
What does pH-balanced mean for a wound cleanser?
Healthy skin and wound tissue sit in a mildly acidic range, while tap water and many soaps are more alkaline and can disrupt this environment. A pH-balanced (physiological) cleanser is formulated close to the wound's natural pH, minimising irritation and supporting rather than disrupting the conditions the wound needs to heal.
